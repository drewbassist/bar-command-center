February 2012 California Bar · Question 2

Constitutional Law | Selected Answers A and B | Issue spotting, rules, application, and recall

1. Fact pattern summary

City opened a new central bus station and provided a large bulletin board for free posting of documents. Because the population is approximately half English-speaking and half Spanish-speaking, City requires every free-posted document to appear in both English and Spanish. America for Americans Organization (AAO) sought to post an English-only flyer describing its goal of restricting immigration, announcing meeting times and locations, and soliciting $10 memberships. City refused because the flyer was not bilingual. The question asks whether that refusal violates AAO members’ First Amendment rights.

2. Issues tested, in order

  1. Government action and protected expression
  2. Standing and justiciability
  3. Forum classification
  4. Content and viewpoint neutrality
  5. Public-forum time, place and manner test
  6. Nonpublic or limited forum alternative
  7. Compelled speech and translation burden
  8. Alternative communication channels
  9. Membership solicitation and commercial speech
  10. Freedom of association and secondary arguments

3–5. Issue spotting, rules, and application

1. Government action and protected expression

Spot it: City operates the station and rejects AAO’s flyer.

Rule to memorize: The First Amendment restricts governmental interference with protected expression and applies to local government through the Fourteenth Amendment.

Application: The refusal is governmental action. A flyer describing political objectives and meetings is protected expression.

2. Standing and justiciability

Spot it: City has already refused AAO permission to post.

Rule to memorize: Standing requires concrete injury, causation and redressability. An association may assert members’ rights when members have standing, interests are germane, and individual participation is unnecessary.

Application: Denial creates a present injury traceable to City; allowing the posting could redress it. Answer B additionally addresses ripeness and mootness; no speculative future penalty is needed.

3. Forum classification

Spot it: City makes a large bulletin board available for free posting inside a bus station.

Rule to memorize: Traditional public forums include streets and parks. A designated public forum is government property intentionally opened for general expressive activity; a limited public forum is opened for defined speakers or subjects; other government property may be nonpublic. Classification depends on the government’s intent and the scope of access.

Application: The bus station as a whole is not necessarily a public forum. The unrestricted free-posting board strongly supports a designated public forum; a limited/nonpublic classification must also be argued. Both answers favor treating the board as opened to public expression.

4. Content and viewpoint neutrality

Spot it: AAO advocates restricting immigration; City requires English and Spanish on every free-posted document.

Rule to memorize: Content-based restrictions generally trigger strict scrutiny in public forums; viewpoint discrimination is especially suspect. A facially uniform language requirement may regulate the manner of presentation, but examine its actual operation and purpose.

Application: The rule applies regardless of support for or opposition to immigration. Both answers view it as content/viewpoint neutral; AAO can argue compelled translation burdens its chosen expression.

5. Public-forum time, place and manner test

Spot it: Posting is free but conditional on including both languages.

Rule to memorize: In a traditional or designated public forum, a content-neutral time, place and manner rule must be narrowly tailored to serve a significant governmental interest and leave open ample alternative channels. It need not be the least restrictive means.

Application: City points to equal English- and Spanish-speaking populations and accessibility at the central station. AAO argues translation cost, compelled additional speech and less burdensome approaches. Answers A and B predict the rule survives.

6. Nonpublic or limited forum alternative

Spot it: The board is within transportation facilities rather than a traditional street or park.

Rule to memorize: In a nonpublic forum, or a limited forum for speech outside its permitted scope, restrictions generally must be reasonable in light of forum purposes and viewpoint neutral.

Application: A uniform bilingual requirement is plausibly reasonable to make postings accessible and does not single out AAO’s viewpoint. Both answers conclude City would likely prevail under this alternative.

7. Compelled speech and translation burden

Spot it: AAO wants its own message in English only; compliance requires a Spanish version.

Rule to memorize: The First Amendment protects against certain government-compelled expression. Analyze whether mandatory translation compels an ideological message or instead imposes a neutral condition on use of a government posting facility.

Application: AAO has a meaningful compelled-speech objection, but translation conveys the same message and the board is optional. The selected answers largely analyze this as a manner restriction rather than a separate compelled-speech doctrine.

8. Alternative communication channels

Spot it: Only this free-posting board is identified as restricted.

Rule to memorize: A valid time, place and manner rule in a public forum must leave ample alternative channels, not necessarily an identical free forum.

Application: Answer A mentions other bulletin boards and hand-distribution; Answer B says the group may communicate its message bilingually. The question does not establish that another particular board exists, so avoid asserting one as fact.

9. Membership solicitation and commercial speech

Spot it: The flyer announces meetings and offers memberships for $10.

Rule to memorize: Political and associational advocacy retains strong First Amendment protection; incidental solicitation does not automatically convert the entire communication into ordinary commercial advertising.

Application: Both answers consider solicitation. Neither treats the $10 invitation as sufficient to defeat protection; Answer B distinguishes passive membership invitations from in-person demands for money.

10. Freedom of association and secondary arguments

Spot it: AAO is an organization recruiting members for immigration-policy advocacy.

Rule to memorize: Expressive association is protected. Vagueness requires insufficient notice; overbreadth concerns substantial suppression of protected expression. Prior-restraint concerns require attention to licensing or censorship mechanisms.

Application: Answer B additionally discusses association, vagueness, overbreadth, prior restraint and symbolic speech. It rejects these objections. The flyer is ordinary written expression, not meaningfully a symbolic-conduct case.

6. Exam traps

  • Classify the bulletin board separately from the bus station as a whole.
  • Do not conflate designated and limited public forums: the scope of permitted speech matters.
  • Political advocacy does not lose full protection simply because membership costs $10.
  • Facial neutrality is not the end of the inquiry: consider purpose, application and compelled expression.
  • Do not invent an available alternative bulletin board; the record does not identify one.
  • A neutral public-forum manner rule requires narrow tailoring, not necessarily the least restrictive means.
  • Do not call written flyers symbolic conduct merely because they communicate a message.
  • The fact of refusal supplies a concrete injury; a future prosecution is not necessary.
  • Selected passing answers are not binding statements of doctrine.

Final disposition

City: Both selected answers conclude that the uniform English-and-Spanish posting condition likely survives First Amendment review, principally as a content-neutral restriction serving communication access.

AAO: Has a cognizable speech claim and can contest forum classification, compelled translation and tailoring, but is unlikely to prevail on the analyses presented in Answers A and B.

Core lesson: Identify the precise forum, classify the speech restriction, apply the corresponding level of review, and address alternative arguments without treating every language condition as automatically viewpoint discriminatory.
