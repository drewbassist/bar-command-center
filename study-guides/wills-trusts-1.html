July 2004 California Bar · Question 3

Wills & Trusts | Selected Answers A and B | Issue spotting, rules, application, and recall

1. Fact pattern summary

Hank lived in State X with daughter Ann, whose mother had died. In 1996 he married Wanda and executed a will giving 5% of his estate in trust to whoever skied with him most in the 12 months preceding death, paid over ten years, and the residue to Wanda if she survived. Wanda was one of two witnesses; State X invalidates wills witnessed by beneficiaries. No trustee was named and Ann was omitted. In 1998 the family moved permanently to California, where Hank adopted Wanda’s minor son Carl. In 2001 a leg injury ended Hank’s skiing; he instead fished frequently with Fred. Hank died in 2003. Wanda claims under the will; Ann and Carl seek intestate shares; Fred requests cy pres. No estate dollar value is stated.

2. Issues tested, in order

  1. Choice of law and attested-will formalities
  2. Interested witness and Wanda’s devise
  3. Intestate shares by property type
  4. Ann as an existing omitted child
  5. Carl as a subsequently adopted child
  6. Creation of testamentary trust
  7. Beneficiary ascertainability and independent significance
  8. Cy pres: private versus charitable trust
  9. Failed trust and residuary disposition

3–5. Issue spotting, rules, and application

1. Choice of law

Spot it: Will executed in State X, but Hank dies domiciled in California.

Rule to memorize: Under Probate Code § 6113, a written will validly executed under a qualifying jurisdiction’s law, including the domicile at death, may be admitted; California attested-will formalities are in § 6110.

Application: Although State X rejects the interested witness, California can recognize the will if California execution requirements are satisfied. The facts imply two witnesses; their other qualifications are assumed.

2. Interested witness

Spot it: Wanda is a beneficiary and one of only two witnesses.

Rule to memorize: Under § 6112, an interested witness does not invalidate the will. With fewer than two disinterested subscribing witnesses, a rebuttable presumption of procurement by duress, menace, fraud, or undue influence applies; unrebutted, the devise is limited by the statutory intestate-share measure.

Application: Wanda is a natural beneficiary as Hank’s spouse, which supports rebuttal but does not automatically prove it. Answer A stresses limitation; B argues for rebuttal. The will itself survives.

3. Intestate shares

Spot it: Wanda survives Hank along with Ann and Carl.

Rule to memorize: Under §§ 6401–6402, the spouse receives the decedent’s community-property share and applicable quasi-community property. With two children, the spouse receives one-third of intestate separate property, and the children one-third each.

Application: Wanda already owns her half of community property. The one-third division applies to Hank’s separate property only to the extent it passes by intestacy, not automatically to the entire estate.

4. Ann’s claim

Spot it: Ann lived with Hank before execution and is not named.

Rule to memorize: § 21620 generally protects children born or adopted after execution; § 21622 may protect a previously existing child unknown to the testator or mistakenly believed dead.

Application: Hank knew Ann and lived with her. No omitted-child share; she can take property passing by intestacy.

5. Carl’s claim

Spot it: Carl was adopted in 1998 after the 1996 will; Wanda is his mother.

Rule to memorize: §§ 21620–21621 protect later-adopted children unless intentional omission appears, qualifying outside provision was intended as a substitute, or the testator had another child and devised substantially all the estate to the omitted child’s other parent.

Application: Carl initially qualifies, but Hank had Ann and left approximately 95% to Wanda, Carl’s mother. The parent-beneficiary exception likely defeats his claim. Ann need not be Wanda’s child. If Wanda’s devise is restricted, discuss consequences rather than assuming the exception’s application is unaffected.

6. Testamentary trust

Spot it: Five percent goes to an unnamed trustee over ten years.

Rule to memorize: A private trust requires intent, identifiable property, lawful purpose, and a definite or ascertainable beneficiary. Failure to name a trustee alone is not fatal; a court may appoint one.

Application: The 5% and installment terms show trust property and intent. The missing trustee can be supplied. The beneficiary condition is the real obstacle.

7. Beneficiary and independent significance

Spot it: The beneficiary must have skied with Hank most in the final twelve months, but Hank stopped skiing in 2001.

Rule to memorize: A beneficiary can be identified by a description or fact independently significant apart from the will, but must satisfy the actual description.

Application: Skiing companionship could ordinarily identify a beneficiary; no one is shown to qualify in the year preceding the 2003 death. Fred’s fishing does not meet the skiing condition. This is not merely a missing-name problem.

8. Cy pres

Spot it: Fred asks the court to substitute fishing for skiing.

Rule to memorize: Cy pres is a remedy for eligible charitable trusts when their charitable purpose cannot be achieved as written, not a general power to rewrite a private beneficiary designation.

Application: Hank’s trust benefits a single private companion rather than a charitable purpose. Fred loses.

9. Failed 5% and residue

Spot it: No skiing beneficiary qualifies, and cy pres fails.

Rule to memorize: A failed private trust may result back to the estate; under § 21111, a failed nonresiduary devise ordinarily passes under an effective residuary disposition.

Application: The 5% does not go to Fred. If Wanda’s residuary gift is effective, it ordinarily captures the failed 5%. If restricted, analyze the resulting succession consequences. Answer A favors intestacy; B emphasizes the residuary gift.

6. Exam traps

  • Invalid in State X does not mean invalid in California.
  • An interested witness affects a devise, not necessarily will validity.
  • Community property and separate property are not distributed identically.
  • An existing omitted child is different from a child adopted after the will.
  • Carl’s omitted-child status must be tested against the parent-beneficiary exception.
  • A missing trustee is curable; an unsatisfied beneficiary condition is not automatically curable.
  • Facts of independent significance do not rewrite skiing as fishing.
  • Cy pres does not apply to this private trust.
  • Check the residuary clause before declaring the failed 5% intestate.

Final disposition

Wanda: Likely takes the residue and failed 5% if she rebuts the interested-witness presumption. Ann: No omitted-child share; may inherit intestate property. Carl: Likely excluded by the parent-beneficiary exception; may inherit intestate property. Fred: No recovery.
